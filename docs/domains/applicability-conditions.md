# Applicability conditions

| Model | Condition | Quantity | Bound | Physical justification | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `thermal.lumped.first_order_capacity` | `biot_number` | Bi = h·L_c/k, with L_c declared or V/A_s and h = hA/A_s | ≤ 0.1 (`LUMPED_BIOT_LIMIT`) | Bi is the temperature drop inside the body over the drop across its surface film, and this model asserts the internal drop is zero. At Bi ≤ 0.1 the internal drop stays within about 10 % of the surface drop, so a single body temperature is defensible. | Incropera, DeWitt, Bergman & Lavine, *Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer*, 6th ed. (Wiley, 2007), §5.1, Eq. 5.10 |
| `thermal.lumped.first_order_capacity` | `internal_fourier_number` | Fo = (t/τ)/Bi, τ = C/hA | ≥ 0.2 (`LUMPED_MIN_FOURIER_NUMBER`) | Above Fo ≈ 0.2 the higher modes of the exact series solution have decayed and the body responds as one exponential, which is all a single-capacity model can produce. Below it the body has several time scales, however small Bi is. | Incropera et al., 6th ed. (2007), §5.5.2 (one-term approximation, ≈2 % for Fo > 0.2), with the identity Bi·Fo = t/τ from §5.2, Eq. 5.12 |
| `thermal.lumped.first_order_capacity` | `conductance_excursion_ratio` | max\|T − T_amb\| ÷ declared constant-hA span; 0 under a declared forced regime | ≤ 1 (`EXCURSION_BUDGET_LIMIT`) | Under free convection the surface coefficient follows the driving difference, h ~ ΔT^(1/4) for a laminar external layer, so a constant hA holds only over a stated span. Under forced convection the flow rather than the buoyancy sets h, and the ΔT dependence this condition bounds is absent. | Incropera et al., 6th ed. (2007), §9.2, Eq. 9.19 ff. (free-convection Nu ~ Ra^(1/4)) |
| `thermal.lumped.first_order_capacity` | `capacity_excursion_ratio` | \|T_ss − T_0\| ÷ declared constant-C span | ≤ 1 (`EXCURSION_BUDGET_LIMIT`) | C = ρVc_p and the specific heat of a real solid rises measurably over engineering temperature ranges. How much drift a study tolerates is its own choice, so the span is declared per material rather than fixed here. | Incropera et al., 6th ed. (2007), Table A.1, which tabulates c_p at several temperatures for each solid |
| `thermal.lumped.first_order_capacity` | `radiation_to_convection_ratio` | h_r/h, h_r = εσ(T_s+T_sur)(T_s²+T_sur²) at the peak surface temperature | ≤ 0.1 (`RADIATION_NEGLIGIBILITY_LIMIT`) | Radiation and convection act in parallel over the same surface, so their coefficients add and this ratio is the fractional error of the model's declared "no radiation" assumption. The 0.1 ceiling is the same neglected-mechanism convention the text applies to the lumped criterion, and is recorded as a convention. | Incropera et al., 6th ed. (2007), §1.2.3, Eq. 1.9 (h_r); σ = 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, BIPM, *The International System of Units (SI)*, 9th ed. (2019), §2.3.1 |
| `thermal.lumped.first_order_capacity` | `melting_temperature_utilization` | max(T_0, T_ss) ÷ declared phase-change temperature | ≤ 1 (`PHASE_CHANGE_UTILIZATION_LIMIT`) | The balance carries no latent-heat term and no second phase, so above the phase change it is not inaccurate but describing a body that is no longer there. The trajectory is monotone between its endpoints, so the endpoint maximum bounds it exactly. | Incropera et al., 6th ed. (2007), §5.1 (lumped formulation for a single-phase solid) |
| `electrical.material.rated_linear_tcr_resistance` | `linearization_excursion_ratio` | \|T − T_ref\| ÷ material-declared linearization band | ≤ 1 (`LINEARIZATION_BUDGET_LIMIT`) | R(T) = R_ref(1 + α(T − T_ref)) is the first-order Taylor expansion of ρ(T) about T_ref, and the term it drops grows as (T − T_ref)². The coefficient of that term belongs to the material's own ρ(T) curve, so the band is declared per material and never fixed here. | Ashcroft & Mermin, *Solid State Physics* (Holt-Saunders, 1976), Ch. 26 (temperature dependence of the ideal resistivity) |
| `electrical.material.rated_linear_tcr_resistance` | `operating_temperature_utilization` | T ÷ material-declared maximum operating temperature (both absolute) | ≤ 1 (`OPERATING_TEMPERATURE_LIMIT`) | A hard material limit rather than a modelling tolerance: above its rating a conductor is annealing, oxidising or open. No temperature coefficient describes a part that is no longer the part that was specified. | IEC 60115-1, *Fixed resistors for use in electronic equipment — Part 1: Generic specification*, Clause 2 (category temperature and rated dissipation) |
| `electrical.material.rated_linear_tcr_resistance` | `reduced_debye_temperature` | T ÷ material-declared Debye temperature θ_D | ≥ 1/3 (`BLOCH_GRUENEISEN_LINEAR_FLOOR`) | Above roughly this fraction of θ_D the phonon population scattering the conduction electrons is classical and a metal's ideal resistivity is linear in T; well below it Bloch–Grüneisen gives ρ ~ T⁵ and no single coefficient fits. The 1/3 is the conventional engineering reading of "T ≫ θ_D" and is recorded as a convention, not as a printed threshold. | Ashcroft & Mermin, *Solid State Physics* (1976), Ch. 26, Eq. 26.55; Kittel, *Introduction to Solid State Physics*, 8th ed. (Wiley, 2005), Ch. 6 |
| `electrical.material.rated_linear_tcr_resistance` | `linear_resistance_ratio` | 1 + α(T − T_ref) = R(T)/R_ref | > 0, strictly (`MINIMUM_LINEAR_RESISTANCE_RATIO`) | Every straight line with a non-zero slope crosses zero, and past the crossing the expression describes a negative conductor rather than a poor one. The bound is the physics of the computed quantity, so it needs no tolerance and no citation. | The model's own constitutive form; cf. Incropera-style admissibility, and the solver's post-hoc `resistance_strictly_positive` check, which this condition anticipates |
| `electrical.dc.resistor_ohm` | `dissipated_power_utilization` | V·I ÷ (derating × declared rated dissipation) | ≤ 1 (`RATING_UTILIZATION_LIMIT`) | Above its rated dissipation an element's temperature rise carries it out of the tolerance band it was specified in, and the constant, temperature-independent resistance this model assumes is the first thing lost. Rated dissipation is defined against a stated reference ambient, so the derating fraction is a declared input rather than a constant. | IEC 60115-1, Clause 2 (rated dissipation, rated ambient temperature, derating curve) |
| `electrical.dc.resistor_ohm` | `working_voltage_utilization` | \|V\| ÷ (derating × declared maximum working voltage) | ≤ 1 (`RATING_UTILIZATION_LIMIT`) | A second and independent limit: for a high-value element the voltage rating binds long before the dissipation rating does. Exceeding it breaks the element down across its body or coating rather than by dissipation. | IEC 60115-1, Clause 2 (maximum element voltage, specified alongside rated dissipation) |
| `electrical.dc.ideal_voltage_source` | `source_current_utilization` | \|I\| ÷ (derating × declared maximum current) | ≤ 1 (`RATING_UTILIZATION_LIMIT`) | The model's declared assumption is unlimited current compliance, which no real source has. Past its current limit a supply folds back or drops out of regulation, and the terminal voltage stops being the imposed constant this model asserts. | The model's own declared assumption "unlimited current compliance"; limit value supplied per component |
| `electrical.dc.ideal_current_source` | `compliance_voltage_utilization` | \|V_terminal\| ÷ (derating × declared compliance voltage) | ≤ 1 (`RATING_UTILIZATION_LIMIT`) | The dual of the voltage source's limit and the direct falsification of this model's declared unlimited compliance voltage. Above its compliance range the delivered current is set by the network, not by the source. | The model's own declared assumption "unlimited compliance voltage"; limit value supplied per component |
